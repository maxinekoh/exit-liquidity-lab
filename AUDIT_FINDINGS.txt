TECHNICAL AND ACADEMIC SCRUTINY REVIEW
Exit Liquidity Lab | SC6106 | 13 November 2026

VERDICT
Conditionally defensible as a narrowly scoped educational allocation experiment. Not production-ready. Not an independent security audit, a novel DeFi protocol, an empirical proof of fairness, or evidence of profitable yield farming. Actual MetaMask-extension testing, Sepolia evidence, human usability work and any required institutional authorship declaration remain outstanding.

VERIFIED RESULTS
34 regression assertions passed. An independent state-machine oracle exercised 480 actions over six deterministic seeds, including 339 mined reverted transactions; 17,613 assertions checked state, reservations, entitlements and conservation. A separate 32-user case with unequal base-unit requests and 17 funding installments recovered all principal and terminal dust. The policy model checked 901 scenario/permutation pairs. Six HTTP cases and four wallet-error scenarios passed in Chrome with simulated EIP-1193 responses. Full deposit-to-partial-claim workflow passed against the local backend. A clean npm ci installation was tested separately. Machine-readable evidence is under evidence/.

FINDINGS AND DISPOSITION
1. Development toolchain vulnerabilities: HIGH residual operational risk. Final npm audit records 33 advisories including five critical, confined to Ganache's bundled subtree. Patched ethers to 6.17.0 and compiler tooling to solc 0.8.38 with tmp 0.2.7 override. Development-only classification is containment, not remediation. npm omit-dev advisory output still includes bundled entries; do not claim a zero-advisory audit. Use localhost, disposable test keys and no real funds. Replace legacy local-chain tooling before production work.

2. Arithmetic bound: FIXED. The earlier design used q*F without an aggregate bound. MAX_PRINCIPAL=10^18 base units now caps all outstanding principal. A round request q and funded F cannot exceed it, so q*F <=10^36 <2^256. Donations do not enlarge Q or F beyond requested principal. Existing requests plus new deposits remain within the cap. The fixed-token assumption is necessary.

3. Cross-cohort starvation: HIGH economic limitation, acknowledged rather than silently redesigned. The next request cohort opens only after full funding of the active cohort. A manager withholding fixture funds can block new requests indefinitely. There is no forced repayment or guaranteed exit. This prevents a production-readiness verdict. Tests establish allocation safety, not eventual liveness.

4. Arbitrary-token behavior: UNSUPPORTED. Deposit balance-delta checking is not a universal defense against malicious, rebasing, fee-on-transfer or callback tokens. No malicious-token campaign was completed. Deployment intentionally uses the supplied conventional demo asset. Its unrestricted faucet prevents any economic-value claim.

5. Benchmark weakness: FIXED IN MODEL. Replaced fractional arithmetic and one-path assumptions with integer cumulative funding and robustness checks for zero demand, no return, excess return and six permutations. Only a single eligible cohort is modeled. Arrival responses, strategic waiting, external protocol competition and multi-cohort welfare are not tested. Equality of proportional percentages follows from the policy; it is not evidence of universal fairness or higher total recovery.

6. Backend robustness: HARDENED. Reject wrong chain and absent vault bytecode at startup. Read state at a common block, return 404 for missing assets, reject non-GET methods and read files before writing response headers. No persistent indexer, rate limit, authentication session, reorg reconciliation or public hosting is claimed. State-changing authority remains on-chain.

7. UI evidence: IMPROVED. Explicit per-user pending-liquidity field added. Missing wallet, wrong chain, rejected connection and rejected signature were tested with simulated wallet responses. Real MetaMask testing remains separate. Browser successful workflow is real local chain execution, not a static mockup.

8. Test strength: IMPROVED. Original negative cases often used eth_call simulations; the stress oracle additionally inspects mined revert receipts and verifies unchanged model state. Seeds and bounds are fixed and reproducible, not exhaustive coverage or proof. 32 users is a bounded scale observation, not mainnet-throughput evidence.

ACADEMIC CLAIM REVIEW
The Aave documentation and Merge-era empirical paper support withdrawal liquidity constraints. The SNX governance record is supplementary evidence, not an estimate of failed withdrawals. Sun et al. is labelled an academic preprint. The IMF sample is historical. Existing ERC-7540, Morpho and Yearn mechanisms are acknowledged; Yearn's strategy order is distinguished from depositor allocation. The prototype does not reanalyze historical raw data, replay the Merge or prove how those users would have fared under this policy. All benchmark data are synthetic. Blockchain can enforce a transparent pooled-fund policy; that does not prove it is the commercially preferred policy.

Likely reviewer objections and defensible responses:
- Why not a database? It can record requests, but cannot independently enforce transfer permissions over on-chain pooled assets. The contract supplies that boundary.
- Why not ERC-7540? This prototype studies policy within a reduced principal-only model and does not claim standards compliance. Full standard support is future integration work.
- Is it fair? Equal funded proportions within a fixed cohort are a specified property. Across cohorts it gives priority to earlier requests and may cause starvation.
- Does it solve a bank run? No. It allocates returned liquidity. It cannot create assets, compel debt repayment or ensure solvency.
- Is the yield strategy real? No. Fixture return timing is controlled. A real lending adapter and gain/loss-aware share accounting remain portfolio extensions.
- Can the manager steal? The fixture's release destination is fixed to the vault; the manager can nevertheless withhold funds indefinitely. Financial control and liveness must be assessed separately.

RUBRIC AND PORTFOLIO ASSESSMENT
Problem analysis, contract rules, backend integration and reproducible testing are now stronger evidence. Functionality still lacks actual MetaMask and public-network acceptance. UI lacks measured human usability. The report stays within eight pages and identifies limitations. No mark can be guaranteed from category weights alone. For a DeFi engineering portfolio, add one pinned-fork lending adapter and yield/loss accounting before describing this as yield infrastructure beyond a principal-only experiment.
